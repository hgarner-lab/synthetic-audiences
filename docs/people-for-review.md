# The 24 people: names for review

The synthetic people in this prototype are Chinese senior decision-makers. Their names, gender and
age range are a first draft written without a native speaker. Please review before this goes in
front of clients.

## How the list was built

- 12 women and 12 men. Every role (checkers, gatekeepers, spreaders, reshapers) has 3 of each, and
  every group has 2 of each, so no role or group leans one way.
- Surname first, as in China. The app shows full names, and the Chinese characters on each profile.
- Given names chosen to suit people born roughly 1965–1985, with a mix of one- and two-character
  names, clear about gender in characters, and no repeats.
- Faces match each person's gender and age (grey or greying hair for people 50 and over). Glasses
  and hairstyles are spread across people so looks don't line up with roles.

## Changes after the first check

A check against public records found five names that exactly matched well-known people
(a singer, a former head of the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, an actress, an Olympic
champion and an entertainer). They were changed, keeping the same surname, gender and
generational feel:

| Was | Now |
| --- | --- |
| He Jie 何洁 | He Wenjie 何文洁 |
| Ren Zhigang 任志刚 | Ren Zhiming 任志明 |
| Peng Dan 彭丹 | Peng Xiaodan 彭晓丹 |
| Tang Lin 唐琳 | Tang Yilin 唐怡琳 |
| Shi Hui 石慧 | Shi Wenhui 石文慧 |

The other names were kept on purpose. Names that feel slightly of their generation suit
senior decision-makers in their 40s, 50s and 60s.

## Questions for reviewers

1. Does each name sound natural for a senior professional of that age? None should feel dated,
   trendy or invented.
2. Do any match a well-known public figure?
3. Are the characters' meanings positive or neutral, and is the gender clear in each?
4. Surname first is right in China. For an international audience, would given name first be
   better in English?
5. Do the faces look right for Chinese professionals? Anything that reads as a caricature or a
   stereotype should be changed.
